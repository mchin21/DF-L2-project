# Directed Forgetting and Second/Additional Language Learning Project

## Preliminary data analysis
For my final project, I created code to perform preliminary analysis on data collected for my first qualifying exam in the Linguistics department. The experiment is composed of a directed forgetting paradigm and a language background questionnaire. Results from 16 participants are displayed in a Jupyter notebook, showing the hit rates (old items correctly identified as being old) and false alarms (new items incorrectly identified as being old) for my two participant groups: English native speakers and English second/additional language learners. The notebook outputs data visualizations for both analyses as well as the average scores and standard deviations for each participant group.

## 250 word abstract:
Forgetting can be beneficial in many contexts, and perhaps unintuitively, in second/additional language (L2) learning. L2 learners have been shown to have a deficit in comparison to native speakers of integrating information that has been shown to be incorrect or unacceptable into their linguistic knowledge (Tachihara & Goldberg, 2025). This study explores if this deficit originates when learners are asked to forget information by measuring English second language (ESL) learners’ ability to forget words of varying familiarity using an online study. Participants will complete a directed forgetting task, wherein the training phase, participants will be exposed to a mix of easy and difficult 5-7 letter English words, each followed by a cue to either remember the word for the testing phase later in the study (R) or a cue to forget the word (F). In the testing phase, participants will be asked if they recognize new easy and difficult English words, as well as words that were included during the training phase regardless of their associated remember/forget cue. They will also complete an English LexTALE task to measure English proficiency (Lemhöfer & Broersma, 2012), an Author Recognition Task to mesure English print exposure (Stanovich & West, 2017), and a language background questionaire. This study is within-subjects with a 2x2 factorial design. Data will be analyzed by English or non-English L1 group. By studying L2 learners’ ability to forget information when explicitly tasked to, we can uncover if word difficulty is a factor when integrating new information into their linguistic knowledge.
